Challenge

Launch a mystery-box platform that feels as exciting as a casino but as trustworthy as a store — for casual players and resellers alike, on web and mobile.

Results

+12% visit → first box opened
−25% odds-related support tickets

The product earns on every box opened

The margin is built into each box's prize pool, so the growth model is simple: turn a visitor into someone who opens their first box, then give them a reason to open the next one. Every design decision was measured against two numbers — first-open conversion and boxes per user.

Problem

Mystery boxes have a reputation: hidden odds, rigged drops, prizes that never arrive. Users come in curious and leave suspicious. If the interface doesn't prove fairness on every screen, nobody risks their first €10.

What users told us

Survey of 120 target users + 12 in-depth interviews

The problem wasn't excitement. It was proof.

I started with a briefing and secondary research of 6 competing platforms, then moved to interviews and a survey. Competitors had plenty of hype — confetti, sounds, flashing prizes. What none of them had was a convincing answer to «can I trust this?». That became the design brief.

Key insight

Half the audience plays for the thrill. The other half plays for ROI.

One interface had to give Alexa emotion and Jordan evidence — at the same time.

Not every feature deserves a sprint

With a team of five, I ran a Kano analysis of the candidate features and mapped them on value vs. effort. Box classes turned out to be a basic need — without them nothing else matters. Odds display and live drops were delighters. Sound on opening was a reverse feature: it irritates active users, so it became optional.

Feature Category Why it matters

Filtering & sorting Performance The faster and more precise, the more satisfied users are

Solution

Three decisions that built trust

01 · Clear odds

Odds you can read in two seconds

Every box shows its full prize list with drop rates, plus a «Provably fair» link right next to the Open button. Odds stopped being fine print and became part of the product: the higher the price, the better the chance — and users can see it.

02 · Live drops

Social proof beats any «100% fair» badge

A live feed of real openings runs across the top of the home page. Seeing that someone just won a Rolex does more for trust than any promise in the footer — and it pulls new visitors straight into the featured boxes.

03 · Simplified UI

Value you understand without reading

Boxes are split into 10 color-coded classes, from €2 to €40,000. Color tells you the price tier before you read a single number: Alexa scans, Jordan filters.

One navigation for novices and pros

Six top-level sections and a shallow two-level hierarchy. A new user reaches their first box in 2 taps; a power user gets to battles and the wallet without digging through menus.

Retention

The first box is acquisition. The second one is the business.

Trust brings users in; mechanics bring them back. I designed three loops, each tied to a need from the interviews:

  • Battles — users open the same boxes head-to-head, the winner takes everything. Pure emotion for Alexa.
  • Leaderboard — monthly prizes for the top players keep the competitive core active.
  • Ranks — seven levels from Starter to Diamond that grow with activity. Recognition and status — exactly what Jordan asked for.

Gamification

Progress you can see is progress you want to continue

To grow engagement, we added a gamification layer: ranks and a monthly leaderboard. For a product designer this isn't decoration — it's a way to turn single visits into a habit:

  • Visible progress. People are more motivated the closer they see themselves to the next level (goal-gradient effect). A rank bar gives every opening a meaning beyond the prize itself.
  • Status and comparison. A leaderboard and rank badges let users see where they stand among others — a strong driver for a competitive, collector-type audience.
  • A reason to come back. Monthly resets and new ranks create regular return triggers, which directly supports retention and the number of boxes per user.

Mobile is where most boxes are opened

Most of the audience plays from a phone, so mobile got its own layout rather than a squeezed desktop: a box can be opened one-handed and the result lands in the thumb zone.

Compliance

Regulation is part of the interface

Gambling products work under different laws in every country, so in some cases the interface had to adapt to legal requirements. Instead of a generic error, a user from a restricted region gets a clear, on-brand screen: what happened, that we're working on it, and how to stay in touch — a dead end turned into a future comeback.

A brand that stands out in a flashy market

Competitors shout with neon and casino gold. LuxBox speaks calmly: monospace type, a 12-column grid, soft gradient noise and a strict palette — it looks like a premium store, not a slot machine. ~60 components cut design-to-dev handoff time roughly in half.

Design didn't stop at the product

Together with our marketer I built a template system for launch campaigns and social media, so the brand stayed consistent from the first ad to the box opening screen.
